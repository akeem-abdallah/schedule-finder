Fitting the whole grid on a phone

Short answer: yes for 4–5 day weeks down to 320px, and that covers every real AURAK schedule. 6 days needs 360px. 7 days genuinely doesn't fit — the honest limit is at the bottom.

1 — The budget

Starting from the viewport: −16px page padding (dropped from 20 to 8 each side on mobile), −2px board border, −36px time gutter. Whatever's left is split between day columns.

At 320px the instructor line is gone; at 390px there's room for a surname. That's the only content difference between them — and it's one media query, no JS.

3 — The five changes that make it fit

1. Columns become minmax(0, 1fr) instead of fixed 140px. This is the whole trick, and there's a trap in it worth knowing: plain 1fr does not work here. 1fr is shorthand for minmax(auto, 1fr), and that auto floor means the column refuses to shrink below its widest piece of content — so one long course code silently forces the grid wider than the screen and you get horizontal overflow anyway. minmax(0, 1fr) sets the floor to zero and lets the column actually shrink. Your inline style becomes something like `${gutter}px repeat(${activeDays.length}, minmax(0, 1fr))`.

2. Every grid cell and block needs overflow: hidden. Even with a zero floor, content that's too wide will paint outside its cell. Clipping it is what keeps the columns honest.

3. The gutter shrinks and the label abbreviates. 110px → 36–40px, and 9:00 AM → 9 AM. Still 12-hour — it's only dropping :00, which is always zero on an hour line anyway. That's a display change in the grid gutter only; to12Hour() stays exactly as it is for the blocks and the section list.

4. Block content sheds lines as space runs out. Desktop shows code, instructor, time. Phone shows subject over code-section only. The time is genuinely redundant in a grid — the block's vertical position already encodes it, which is the entire point of a timetable. Instructor is the real loss, and it's still one tap away in the section picker.

5. Page padding 20px → 8px on mobile. Buys 24px, which is most of a sixth column at 320px.

All five are CSS. Point 4 renders all three lines always and hides two of them in the media query — so there's no resize listener, no width in state, no JSX change at all for the responsive behavior. The only JSX edit is swapping the fixed column widths in that inline gridTemplateColumns string.

4 — Where it honestly stops

7 days at 320px gives 38px per column — 26px of text space after overhead. 104-01 needs 33px at 9px type. It does not fit, and dropping to 7px type isn't a fix, it's unreadable. 6 days at 320px is 44px, which works but is tight.

Whether that matters depends on your data. The UAE working week is Monday–Friday, and your App.jsx only widens the range past Thursday when a class actually falls there — so a 7-column grid needs a real Sunday class to exist. Your current fake data tops out at Friday. Worth checking against the real 700 rows when they land rather than engineering for it now.

If a 6–7 day week does turn up, the cheap fallback is a media query at max-width: 340px that re-enables horizontal scroll for that case only — every other phone still shows the full week. That's a few lines, and it degrades rather than breaking.

The other option, which most mobile calendar apps actually use, is an agenda view on phones — a vertical list grouped by day instead of a grid. It reads better at 320px than any grid can. But it's a different view to build and maintain, and you asked for the grid, so it's noted rather than proposed.
