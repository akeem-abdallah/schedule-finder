The board

Two type families doing different jobs: monospace for every piece of data (codes, sections, times, counts) so columns align on their own, and system sans for prose and labels. Ruled rows instead of cards. Zero radius. One accent.

The rules that make it this and not something else

Mono is for data, sans is for language. Subject, code, section counts, times, the result counter — monospace, so CSCI/MATH/PHYS and 112/113/095 line up into columns without you setting a single width. Course titles, button labels, headings — system sans. Mixing the two on purpose is most of the personality here.

Rules, not boxes. One hairline between rows, one heavy 2px rule under the status strip. No cards, no shadows, no radius anywhere. The heavy/hairline contrast is the whole hierarchy.

Numbers get to be big. 003 / 048 at 20px mono is the loudest thing in the results view, because the count is what you came for. Leading zeros are deliberate — they keep the width fixed as you page, so nothing shifts.

Labels are tiny, tracked, and uppercase. 9–10px, letter-spacing: .13em, muted. This is what lets them be small without looking like an accident.

Course color is one hue at alpha — same mechanism as before, so it inverts for free. Text inside blocks stays the normal text color; only the left rule and the wash carry identity.

Two judgment calls to confirm or reject:

1. 24-hour times in the grid gutter (0900, 1100, 1300). Fixed-width, no AM/PM, aligns perfectly — very timetable. But it's a functional change to to12Hour(), and AURAK students may just read 12-hour faster. Easy to keep 12-hour and still get alignment from the mono font.

2. The status strip replaces the big centered <h1>. The title goes small, tracked, top-left, with UNOFFICIAL as a filled chip and the update time in mono beside it. That kills the 34px centered heading you have now — which is the single most generic element on the page, but it is a real change in feel.
