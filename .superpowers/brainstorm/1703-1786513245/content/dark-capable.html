Three directions built to flip

Each shown light and dark — same design, same values, one theme swap. All three use the same three mechanisms (explained at the bottom) that make the dark theme almost free instead of a second design.

A — Blueprint

Defined entirely by contrast and rules, never by tint. Nothing has a shadow. Because the design is relationships (ink-on-paper, thick rule vs hairline), flipping it is literally swapping two values — this is the direction that inverts most cleanly of anything here.

B — Elevated

Surfaces stack. In light the card sits above the page with a shadow; in dark it sits above by being lighter than the page — that's how real dark UIs signal depth, since shadows are invisible on near-black. Cool near-neutrals, one periwinkle accent that holds its saturation on both.

C — Warm low-contrast

Neither theme is harsh — light is warm off-white rather than white, dark is warm charcoal rather than black. Lower contrast throughout, so the only real saturation on screen is the courses themselves. The calmest of the three to look at for a long registration session.

Why these flip and the earlier ones didn't

1. Course blocks carry one hue, not a pastel. The earlier design gave every course a hardcoded pale fill like #eef2ff. A pastel has no dark counterpart, so dark mode needs a second full set of eight. Here the fill is the hue at low alpha — rgba(47,107,255,.10) over a light surface is a pale wash, and the same hue at .22 over a dark surface is a deep one. One value per course, both themes.

2. Block text uses the normal text color, not a per-course dark shade. The identity comes from the left rule and the wash, so the words just use --text / --text-muted like everything else. That deletes eight more color decisions and guarantees contrast in both themes.

3. Hairlines are alpha, not hex. rgba(255,255,255,.08) reads correctly over any surface underneath it. A fixed #e2e8f0 border only works on one background — put it on a dark card and it glows.

Device rules that apply to all three: every tappable thing gets a 44×44px minimum hit area (your current x is about 20px — genuinely hard to hit on a phone); headings use clamp() so they scale with the viewport instead of jumping at a breakpoint; and the weekly grid lives in its own horizontally-scrolling container with the time gutter pinned left, because 5 days × readable columns will never fit 375px.

A

Blueprint

Most personality, best-reading grid, cleanest inversion. Reads confident/technical. Risk: severe if you wanted warm.

B

Elevated

The "real product" look, and the most familiar to students. Risk: closest of the three to generic — the accent and warmth of the neutrals are what save it.

C

Warm low-contrast

Easiest on the eyes, most distinctive dark theme (warm charcoal, not black). Risk: low contrast can read washed-out on a cheap phone screen in sunlight.
